Camera guide

Which type of CCTV camera do you need?

Most systems mix two or three types. Here’s where each one earns its place.

Dome

Best for
Indoor rooms, shops, offices, lobbies and covered sit-outs.
Strengths
Discreet and vandal-resistant; people can’t easily tell where it’s pointing.
Watch out for
The dome cover can reflect infrared at night if it’s dirty or under eaves.
Shop dome cameras

Bullet

Best for
Gates, boundary walls, parking and long driveways.
Strengths
Long infrared range and a visible deterrent; easy to aim at distant areas.
Watch out for
More exposed to tampering — mount high, out of reach.
Shop bullet cameras

Turret (eyeball)

Best for
Entrances, sit-outs and house exteriors — a good all-rounder.
Strengths
No dome glare at night; easy to re-angle; compact.
Watch out for
Shorter range than a bullet of the same class.
Shop turret cameras

PTZ (pan-tilt-zoom)

Best for
Factory yards, campuses, large car parks and warehouses.
Strengths
One camera patrols a wide area and zooms in on incidents.
Watch out for
It only records where it’s pointing — pair it with fixed cameras.
Shop PTZ cameras

Fisheye (360°)

Best for
Open-plan shops, halls and ceilings where one camera should see the whole room.
Strengths
Replaces several fixed cameras indoors; no blind corners.
Watch out for
Less detail at the edges — not ideal for identifying faces at doors.
Shop fisheye cameras

Wi-Fi / wireless

Best for
Finished homes and rented spaces where running cables is difficult.
Strengths
Quick to install; most record to an SD card and the cloud as well.
Watch out for
Still need power at each point; signal through thick walls can drop.
Shop Wi-Fi cameras

4G & solar

Best for
Farms, construction sites and plots with no power or internet.
Strengths
Fully independent: solar panel, battery and a SIM card for live view.
Watch out for
Needs good mobile coverage and a data plan; monsoon months reduce solar charging.
Shop 4G / solar cameras

Video door phone

Best for
Main doors and gates: see and talk to visitors before opening.
Strengths
Indoor monitor, two-way talk and, with some models, gate release and mobile calls.
Watch out for
Not a substitute for recording cameras around the property.
Shop video door phones

Rules of thumb

  • Mix wide and narrow: wide lenses (2.8 mm) show the scene, narrower lenses (6–12 mm) at doors and gates catch faces and number plates.
  • Mount at 2.5–3 m: high enough to resist tampering, low enough to see faces, not just the tops of heads.
  • Avoid pointing at the sun or bright lights: backlight turns visitors into silhouettes; WDR cameras help.
  • Plan storage up front: use the storage calculator so footage isn’t overwritten before you need it.
